# Day Trading Attention skill

Standard `SKILL.md` bundle. Loads in Claude Code, Codex, and Grok.

Skill root = the directory that contains this file. Read `references/daytrading-attention.md` relative to that root. Invoke as `/garyvee-daytrading-attention-skill` (Claude, Grok) or `$garyvee-daytrading-attention-skill` (Codex).

You are running Day Trading Attention as a procedure, not summarizing the book.

**Rule 1:** creative is the variable of success — not follower count, not budget. The interest graph distributes whatever people actually want to watch, so relevance = reach, and only proven content gets amplified.

**Rule 2:** do not boil the ocean. Deliver the next actionable step for wherever the subject actually is, not the whole framework at once.

## Plan

Order is mandatory. Do not skip to posting cadence.

1. **Cohorts** — 3–8 cohorts with teeth (e.g., not "moms 30–45" but "40–45-year-old moms in Chattanooga into sustainable clothing"). Volume over breadth. They are hypotheses, not cages — an accordion.
2. **PAC** — audit what those cohorts actually consume: search each cohort on TikTok/Reels/LinkedIn search, note formats, audio, titles, and overperformers. Culture = signals (stats, news, memes, influencers, consumer behaviors). Authenticity rule: if the subject is not funny, do not force funny.
3. **SOC** — draft strategic organic content per cohort. Every piece must pass: hook in the first three seconds, no "does this feel like an ad?" feeling, native to the platform (creative unit, in-app features, per-platform copy), one emotion or story. Volume: 4–5 pieces per platform per day is the target shape; scale to what the subject can sustain.
4. **Amplification** — only after organic overperformance. Find the overindex (a post that beat the account's normal range), tweak it sales-focused, spend small first (e.g., $100 per post), scale only the winners next cycle. Broad targeting + great creative is usually better than manual narrow.
5. **Modern commercials** — only if resources allow: one higher-production, broadly relatable video concept built from the top validated SOC insights (sawdust: capture extra footage during any shoot). Never from boardroom guesses.
6. **PCS** — close the loop: read comments, refine cohorts, and name the next 3 pieces of content from what was learned.

End with the next 3 actions, ordered SPECIAL.

## Audit

Score each as **solid / leak / missing**. Cite a live post, line, or metric. No vibes without a quote or number.

- Cohorts: is there a defined audience with teeth, or is the content generic (vanilla for everyone)?
- PAC: does the content reflect platform-native format and culture, or is it copy-pasted cross-platform with no tweaks?
- Hook: does the first thing the viewer sees (title, thumbnail, first 3 seconds) stop the scroll, or is there wasted time before the point?
- Ad-feel: does it read like a commercial/brand-guideline piece, or like content a person would want to watch?
- Volume: is there enough output to learn from, or is the subject "posting once a week and wondering why nothing hits"?
- Amplification: is money going to proven winners, or to "boost everything"?
- Listening: are comments being read and turned into the next piece, or ignored?

Lead with what is true. Then the scorecard. Then 3 next actions, ordered.

## Hook

The hook is the entire first impression. Do not approve a piece whose hook fails.

- First three seconds decide relevance. Title + thumbnail + opening line + who is in frame + the first frame itself.
- No slow starts, no unnecessary frames. Get into the message as fast as possible.
- Title should be a question or a named cohort ("Car Dealership Red Flags, Part 1", "pov: you're a girl dad") — something the viewer self-selects into.
- If the text is on the video, check readability (contrast, position, in-app text tools).
- When in doubt, write 2–3 hook variations and let the subject test all of them. Do not pick by taste.

## Amplify

Never recommend spending on content that has not proven itself organically.

1. Find the account's normal range of views/engagement. An overperformer is anything clearly above that range — it does not need to be viral by someone else's standards.
2. Tweak the winner toward the ask: add a CTA frame, an email, a consultation line, a QR code — but do not bury the content that made it work.
3. Spend small first. Scale only what converted. Watch for ad fatigue: if the same 5 ads are running, the answer is more creative, not more money.
4. Do not dismiss a platform because it "doesn't convert" — algorithms mature (TikTok ads, Facebook ads in 2011). Keep 10–20% of effort on emerging platforms.
5. Prefer micro-influencers and local creators over A-list celebrity endorsements unless the reach is genuinely needed.

## Listen

PCS is the biggest variable in the process)Skip.

1. Read the comments on the subject's posts and on adjacent accounts in the category.
2. Extract human-behavior observations: who is actually responding, what tone, what inside jokes, what unexpected cohort.
3. Turn each observation into an action: a new or adjusted cohort, a new piece of SOC, a PAC signal, or a product/collaboration idea.
4. Engage meaningfully. Comments are content. Generic "cool video" replies do nothing. Never spam hashtags or "follow me."
5. Sources beyond their own posts: platform search, Reddit, Quora. If they are out of content ideas, the answer is more listening.

## Voice

Write like a direct editor. Complete sentences. Lead with the answer. No "post consistently and stay authentic" boilerplate — name the cohort, the hook, and the next post.
