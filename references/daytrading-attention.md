# Day Trading Attention frameworks

Distilled from Gary Vaynerchuk, *Day Trading Attention* (HarperCollins, 2024). This file is the source of truth for the system. `SKILL.md` is the procedure.

## Thesis

Marketing is day trading attention, not buy-and-hold. Understand what attention is, where it is, where it is underpriced, and how to leverage it for brand building and sales.

- **Attention is the game. Underpriced attention is the opportunity.** Attention is an asset like real estate: when a platform has eyes on it and storytelling there is cheap, go all in before prices rise. 1950s TV was underpriced; 2024 TV is generally overpriced; Super Bowl spots remain a bargain.
- **The TikTokification of social media.** Every platform is converging on the **interest graph** (content served by what you are interested in) away from the **social graph** (content from who you follow). Consequence: **relevance = reach.** Creative is the variable of success, not follower count. Platforms are now aligned with advertisers: they distribute content people actually want.
- **Supply and Demand of Content.** Underpriced attention is a land grab that closes. Early TikTok organic reach was obnoxiously high; it takes better content now. Entrepreneurs move first, then small businesses, then big budgets — then the gap closes. Get in early or pay later.
- The stakes: being **consumer-centric** vs **boardroom-centric**. Boardroom-centric (brand guidelines as religion) is the biggest issue facing the biggest brands.

## The Modern Advertising Framework — six core variables

### 1. Cohort development — who you want to reach

- Cohorts = narrow audience segmentations you make content FOR. Do not serve everyone vanilla.
- **Cohorts with teeth**: not "18–35 males" but "18–22-year-old males in NY into basketball."
- **Cohorts are an accordion** — shrink/expand on comment insights; kill cohorts after 1–4 months and stand up new ones.
- Define a **high volume** (20, 30, 40+) — more cohorts = more chances at relevance.
- Consider business objectives; one piece of content can hit multiple cohorts (labels are hypotheses, not cages).
- Do not overthink media addressability — narrow cohorts are an ideation tool; algorithms find the actual audience.
- Consider subcultures, life stages, psychographics, cultural trends — not just age/location/interest.

### 2. Platforms and Culture (PAC)

Two prongs, both mandatory.

**Platforms:** master creative units and features (Reels, carousels, stories, duets, stitches, in-app titles, comment capabilities).

1. Take account of ALL features a platform has.
2. The platform wants users to stay longer — reward it with content people watch.
3. Pay attention to what is UNIQUE to a platform.
4. Pay attention to what is SIMILAR across platforms (signals of direction).
5. Notice how the same user behavior shows up differently per platform.
6. Stay up-to-date on platform tests (AI dubbing, chatbots).
7. Consider the psychology of the user on the platform (LinkedIn = career mind, TikTok = lighthearted, Facebook = friends/family).
8. **Be a practitioner, not a headline reader.**

**Culture:** know how to speak to each narrow cohort — trends, behaviors, slang, memes. Track **signals** (stats, celebrity actions, news, platform trends, debate-sparking content, historic events). Ride trends authentically (Wendy's "Straw Barbie Frosty"); never force it — "this is a game of authenticity." Culture can also be driven through collectibles, collaborations, and event activations.

### 3. Strategic Organic Content (SOC)

SOC = organic content posted to build brand, community, and relevance. The S is deliberate: content must be strategic — "marketing for the sake of better marketing," not spray-and-pray.

- **The hook is everything** — first three seconds decide relevance: title, thumbnail, opening lines, who is in frame, first frame. No wasted frames; get into the message fast.
- Title text, copy (caption), audio (trending audio boosts distribution), creative unit, format, timing, thumbnail design.
- **Does it feel like an ad?** If yes, rework it. Make content for the person consuming it, not for yourself.
- **Does it evoke emotion and tell a story?** Length is irrelevant — "it's not about the length of your content; it's about how good your content is."
- **Is it native to the platform?** Aware of the context of the room you're in: use in-app features, reference platform elements in copy ("share this with your friends" on IG vs "send this to a colleague" on LinkedIn), respect dimensions/grids, per-platform trending audio. Never blind copy-paste across platforms.
- **Volume is non-negotiable** — 4–5 posts per platform per day is the target shape. Do not call a strategy dead before 50–100 days.
- SOC does three jobs: (1) builds brand/relevance from day one, (2) identifies winners to convert into performance ads (brandformance), (3) produces insights that de-risk higher-production commercials.

### 4. Amplification — spending against what works

- **Only amplify proven winners.** Find the organic overindex (e.g., posts average 5–25 views; seven videos hit 500–700 → spend on those). Tweak sales-focused (CTA frames, email, consultation), spend small first ($100 each), scale the winners next month.
- **Creative is the most important variable** in paid media — algorithms do the targeting (Advantage+, Performance Max, interest-graph-led delivery). Broad targeting + great creative usually beats manual narrow; manual layering still has a place (narrow cohorts, first-party data, Nextdoor/Spotify).
- Post–iOS 14.5: attribution is harder → brand building through organic matters more; in-app Shops are the platform answer.
- **Ad fatigue is real**: the lawn-care lesson — "you need fifty-five more ads." Five ads stop working; the work is new creative daily, not more spending.
- **Do not dismiss platforms that "don't convert"** — algorithms mature; keep 10–20% of effort on emerging platforms.
- **Influencer marketing**: prefer micro-influencers, "alpha moms," and emerging creators over A-list celebrity endorsements; the interest graph can make a micro video out-drive a celebrity post.
- Amplification extends to OTT/CTV/FAST (Hulu ~$500/mo for SMBs, Roku, Tubi), YouTube pre-roll, radio, remnant TV. Every channel is overpriced or underpriced depending on execution. TV works when direct-response focused (infomercial-style with QR codes), not for vanilla brand awareness.

### 5. Videos people want to watch — modern commercials

- Generation's "Just Do It" equivalents: Dollar Shave Club's $4,500 video (12,000 orders in 48h), Purple's "Raw Egg Test" (194M views). Videos that evoke emotion and that millions would watch — built on validated SOC insights, not guesses.
- **Sawdust principle**: during any high-production shoot ("cutting wood"), capture maximum additional content ("the sawdust") — behind-the-scenes, interviews, alternate angles — to distribute everywhere.
- Concepts are validated through organic volume first (the Fox Financial "game show" example: a street-interview format that overperformed gets expanded into a produced skit).
- Distribute natively: vertical for TikTok/Reels/Shorts, horizontal for YouTube/CTV; add direct-response elements without being ad-heavy.
- Mascots/characters can carry a brand (Duolingo, GEICO, Flo) — build character personalities over time.

### 6. Post-Creative Strategy (PCS) — listening

- **Reading comments is the biggest variable in the creative process.** Comments are live consumer research. VaynerMedia employs "post-creative strategists" (anthropologists/psychologists) to read every comment.
- Use insights to: refine SOC, uncover new cohorts, find PAC signals, invent new product ideas and collaborations, adjust distribution.
- **Comments are content** — engaging resurfaced posts (especially LinkedIn); build depth in replies; no "cool video" or fire-emoji spam. The $1.80 strategy: leave your two cents on 90 posts a day.
- Sources beyond your own posts: platform search, Reddit, Quora. "If you're running out of content ideas, spend more time listening."

## The loop

```
COHORTS (who) → PAC (platform + culture knowledge) → SOC (strategic organic content at volume)
   → ANALYTICS + COMMENTS (PCS listening) → AMPLIFY winners (paid, small scale first)
   → MODERN COMMERCIALS (produced, de-risked by validated insights) → back to cohorts
```

The canonical worked example (kids' clothing): target "millennial moms" + "girl dads"; search each cohort on TikTok/Reels ("pov: you're a girl dad"); find formats and audio that overperform; make 20–40 variations; post daily; read comments; amplify the winners locally; scale the concept to higher-production content informed by the data.

## Where to start

- **Do not boil the ocean.** "Long before you can dunk a basketball, you must learn how to walk and not shit your pants."
- Start from what comes naturally: cultural-trend instinct OR platform strategy — either is a valid on-ramp. Just start posting; multiple times a day for weeks/months; then layer on more variables.
- Practicing is non-negotiable: "you can't just read about doing push-ups."

## AI forecast

- AI will enable producing hundreds/thousands of content pieces per month and analyzing every variable — making it more important to be a practitioner now.
- AI voice interfaces change consumer behavior: "Send me a Starbucks cold brew" vs "Send me a cold brew" — brands that do not build brand on underpriced attention lose market share to AI assistants and influencer-founded brands.
- Winners: those curious about modern platforms who storytell where attention is underpriced. Losers: those who scoff (Twitter 2006, wine online 1996, Google AdWords early 2000s, TikTok 2019).

## Quick-start order

1. Cohorts with teeth (3–8 for a single subject)
2. PAC audit of those cohorts' actual consumption
3. SOC: hooks + native content at volume
4. Amplify only organic overperformers, small first
5. Modern commercial from validated insights (only if resources allow)
6. PCS: read comments, close the loop, adjust cohorts
